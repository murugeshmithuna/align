# Bicep Curl Elbow Drift

**What it looks like:** The elbow travels forward and away from the torso as
the weight is lifted, instead of staying pinned at the side throughout the
rep. On camera this shows up as the shoulder-elbow-wrist angle opening up
inconsistently with the elbow's position drifting forward relative to the
hip line.

**Why it happens:** Usually the weight is heavier than the biceps alone can
move through the full range, so the front delt and shoulder flexion get
recruited to help swing the elbow forward and shorten the effective range the
biceps have to work through - a load-management compensation, not a random
technical error.

**Why it matters:** Elbow drift quietly turns a bicep-isolation exercise into
a shoulder-assisted movement, reducing the actual training stimulus to the
biceps even though the weight moved and the rep "counted."

**Correction cues:**
- Lightly pin the upper arm against the torso (or brace it against a wall or
  preacher bench) so there's a physical reference point that makes drift
  immediately obvious.
- Drop the weight 10-20% and rebuild strict form before adding load back -
  drift is almost always a sign the working weight has outpaced strict
  range-of-motion control.
- Cue "elbow stays at the hip" out loud on every rep, especially the last
  2-3 reps of a set where drift is most likely to creep in under fatigue.
