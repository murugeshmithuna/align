# Push-Up Neck Alignment

**What it looks like:** The head drops down (chin tucked hard toward the
chest) or cranes upward (looking forward/up toward a mirror or wall) instead
of staying in a neutral position that continues the straight line of the
spine.

**Why it happens:** Usually a habit of watching the floor or watching a
mirror for feedback rather than fixing gaze at a point on the floor roughly
a foot or two ahead of the hands, which naturally keeps the neck neutral.

**Why it matters:** A neutral neck keeps cervical spine loading consistent
with the rest of the plank line; a hard chin tuck or upward craning position
held repeatedly under load can contribute to neck strain over time, and it
often signals the rest of the plank line (hips, back) is also not being
actively held.

**Correction cues:**
- Fix the gaze on a spot on the floor roughly 12-18 inches ahead of the
  hands and hold it there for the whole set - this keeps the neck in a
  naturally neutral position without needing to consciously manage it.
- Think of the head as "part of the plank line," not something separate to
  position - if the rest of the body is a straight line, the head should
  continue that line, not break it.
- Neck position issues frequently co-occur with hip sag - if both appear
  together, treat it as one root cause (core/plank fatigue), not two
  separate problems.
