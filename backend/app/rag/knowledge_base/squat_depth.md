# Squat Depth

**What it looks like:** The rep stops short of the target depth - the hip crease
never drops to or below the top of the knee. On camera this shows up as a
"quarter squat" or "half squat" where the knee angle at the bottom of the rep
stays well above the good-depth threshold (roughly greater than 100-110
degrees of knee flexion, i.e. not bent enough).

**Why it happens:** Most often ankle mobility restriction (the shin can't
travel forward enough to let the hips sink), tight hip flexors, or simply a
protective habit from prior knee/hip discomfort. It can also be intentional
fatigue-avoidance late in a set - depth quietly shrinks as reps accumulate.

**Why it matters:** Partial-depth squats undertrain the glutes and the bottom
range of the quads, and inflate the perceived load (the same weight feels
lighter with a shorter range of motion), which can mislead progressive
overload tracking.

**Correction cues:**
- Widen the stance slightly and let the toes turn out 10-15 degrees to free
  up hip range.
- Elevate the heels slightly (a small plate or squat wedge) if ankle mobility
  is the limiter - this lets the shins travel forward without the heels
  lifting off the ground.
- Cue "sit back and down" rather than "bend the knees" - it recruits the
  hips into the descent instead of turning it into a knee-dominant half-rep.
- If depth degrades specifically in the last 1-2 reps of a set, treat it as a
  fatigue signal, not a mobility issue - consider trimming the set short
  rather than accepting a partial rep.
