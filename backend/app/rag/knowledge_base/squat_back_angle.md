# Squat Back Angle

**What it looks like:** The torso pitches forward past a safe angle relative
to vertical during the descent or the ascent out of the bottom - sometimes
paired with the lower back rounding rather than staying in a neutral arch.
Pose tracking measures this as the trunk's lean angle from vertical at the
rep's hardest point.

**Why it happens:** Commonly a mobility limitation (tight ankles or hips
forcing the torso to lean forward to keep balance), a bar or load path that
sits too far forward, weak core bracing that lets the spine round under load,
or simply a stance/depth combination that biomechanically demands more
forward lean than the lifter can control with a neutral spine.

**Why it matters:** Excess forward lean shifts more of the mechanical load
onto the lower back and hip extensors and away from the quads, and a rounded
lower back under load is one of the more direct mechanisms behind lumbar
strain - the spine loses its ability to transmit force safely once it flexes
out of a neutral arch under compression.

**Correction cues:**
- Brace the core before the descent starts - a full breath held against a
  tight abdominal wall (the "belt of air" cue) keeps the spine rigid through
  the whole rep.
- Check that depth and back angle aren't fighting each other - forcing extra
  depth past the point ankle/hip mobility supports is a common cause of both
  problems appearing on the same rep.
- Keep the bar or load path stacked over the middle of the foot throughout -
  if the load drifts forward, the torso has to lean forward to compensate.
- If forward lean is consistent across every rep (not just the hardest ones),
  treat it as a mobility/technique issue to address in warm-ups, not a
  fatigue pattern.
