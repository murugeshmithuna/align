# Push-Up Hand Placement

**What it looks like:** Hands placed too far forward of the shoulders
(reaching-out position), too far back underneath the chest, or noticeably
wider or narrower than shoulder width in a way that's driving other issues
like hip sag or elbow flare rather than being a problem on its own.

**Why it happens:** Often copied without adjustment from a generic cue, or
drifted into gradually across a set as fatigue sets in and the body looks
for a mechanically easier position.

**Why it matters:** Hand placement is frequently the root cause behind other
flagged issues rather than an independent problem - hands too far forward
mechanically encourages hip sag (the plank line has to bridge a longer gap),
and hands too wide encourages elbow flare.

**Correction cues:**
- Start with hands roughly shoulder-width apart and directly under or just
  slightly ahead of the shoulders, then adjust narrower or wider only in
  small increments based on how it affects elbow tracking and hip position.
- If hand placement drifts forward specifically as a set goes on, treat it
  as a sign the set has gone past the point good form can be sustained.
- When two different issues (e.g. hip sag and elbow flare) show up in the
  same session, check hand placement first - fixing it often resolves both
  at once rather than needing two separate corrections.
