# Push-Up Shallow Depth

**What it looks like:** The chest stops well short of the floor - often only
descending a few inches - before pressing back up, rather than reaching a
true full range where the upper arms approach parallel with the torso or
lower.

**Why it happens:** Most commonly a strength limitation (a full-range push-up
is genuinely harder than a shallow one at the same rep count), but it can
also be a habit built from counting reps rather than range, especially once
fatigue makes full depth uncomfortable.

**Why it matters:** Similar to squat depth, a shallow push-up meaningfully
undertrains the chest and triceps through their full range and inflates how
much a given rep count actually represents in terms of real training
stimulus.

**Correction cues:**
- Use a genuine external depth reference during practice - a small target
  (a rolled towel, a foam block) placed under the chest that must be lightly
  touched each rep removes any ambiguity about how low is low enough.
- Reduce total reps per set rather than shortening range to hit a target
  number - fewer full-range reps train the target muscles more effectively
  than more partial ones.
- If depth shrinks specifically late in a set, that is a normal fatigue
  pattern - ending the set at that point is the correct response, not
  continuing with partial reps.
