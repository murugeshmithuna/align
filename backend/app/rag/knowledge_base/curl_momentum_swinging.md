# Bicep Curl Momentum and Swinging

**What it looks like:** The torso rocks backward and the hips drive forward
to help "throw" the weight upward at the start of the rep, rather than the
biceps alone lifting it from a dead stop. On camera this appears as a sudden
angular velocity spike right at the bottom of the rep that doesn't match a
smooth, muscle-driven contraction.

**Why it happens:** Almost always the weight exceeds what can be strictly
curled for the target rep range, so body momentum substitutes for muscular
force at the hardest part of the range (the bottom, near full elbow
extension).

**Why it matters:** Momentum removes tension from the biceps at exactly the
point in the range where a strict curl is hardest and most productive,
meaning the heaviest-looking sets can actually deliver the least real
muscular stimulus.

**Correction cues:**
- Perform a dead-stop pause (roughly one full second) at the bottom of every
  rep - this makes swinging the weight up physically impossible and forces a
  true muscular start to the concentric.
- Curl facing a wall with the toes an inch from it - any backward rocking
  becomes immediately obvious and self-correcting.
- Reduce the weight until the full set can be completed with a visibly still
  torso before adding load back.
