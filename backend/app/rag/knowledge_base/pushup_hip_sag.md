# Push-Up Hip Sag

**What it looks like:** The hips drop below the line formed by the shoulders
and ankles, so the body forms a "banana" shape instead of a straight plank
line, through part or all of the rep. This is the one push-up form check
ALIGN's live session already measures directly (a straight-line deviation
check alongside the shoulder-elbow-wrist rep-counting angle).

**Why it happens:** Core and lower-back muscles disengaging under fatigue is
the most common cause - the plank position is an active hold, not a passive
one, and it's the first thing to fail as a set gets hard. It can also come
from a hand position that's too far forward of the shoulders, which
mechanically encourages the hips to drop.

**Why it matters:** A sagging hip position loads the lower back in extension
under bodyweight-plus-momentum, which is a common source of lower-back
discomfort from push-ups, and it also reduces how much of the rep's
resistance is actually being driven through the chest and triceps.

**Correction cues:**
- Actively squeeze the glutes and brace the abs before starting the set, as
  if holding a plank - the push-up is a moving plank, not just an arm
  exercise.
- Keep hands roughly under or very slightly in front of the shoulders, not
  reaching far forward.
- If hip sag appears specifically in the last few reps of a set, that's a
  core-endurance fatigue signal - ending the set there preserves better form
  than pushing through with a broken line.
