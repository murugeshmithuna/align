# Bicep Curl Partial Range of Motion

**What it looks like:** The rep starts short of full elbow extension at the
bottom, stops short of full flexion at the top, or both - the visible arc of
motion shrinks compared to a full, strict rep, even though the rep still
gets counted.

**Why it happens:** Fatigue is the most common cause late in a set (partial
reps let a lifter squeeze out extra reps once full range becomes hard), but
it can also be a habit formed from training with a weight that was too heavy
from the start, or a shoulder/elbow mobility restriction limiting the top or
bottom position.

**Why it matters:** The biceps get meaningfully more stimulus from the
stretched (bottom) and fully-contracted (top) portions of the range than from
the middle - cutting either end short specifically removes the part of the
exercise doing the most work.

**Correction cues:**
- Let full elbow extension at the bottom be the non-negotiable rep-start
  position - if that position can't be reached under control, the set is
  effectively over even if a few "reps" are still technically possible.
- Cue a brief squeeze at the top (roughly half a second of peak contraction)
  rather than bouncing straight back down - this also naturally prevents
  swinging into the next rep.
- If range shrinks specifically in the final 1-2 reps of a set, that is a
  normal fatigue signal - the fix is ending the set there, not continuing
  with a shorter range to hit a pre-set rep target.
