# Squat Knee Tracking

**What it looks like:** The knees drift inward toward the midline during the
descent or drive out of the bottom - commonly called "knee valgus" or "knees
caving in." On camera this is measured as the knee's horizontal offset from
the ankle, normalized by hip width - a large inward offset flags the rep.

**Why it happens:** Usually weak or under-activated glute medius (the muscle
responsible for keeping the knee tracking over the foot), a stance that's
narrower than the lifter's natural hip structure, or simply momentary
technical breakdown under a heavy or fatiguing load - valgus collapse often
appears specifically on the hardest rep of a set, not the easiest.

**Why it matters:** Repeated inward knee collapse under load is one of the
more common mechanisms behind knee strain (particularly patellar tendon and
MCL stress), because the knee is absorbing rotational force it isn't
structured to handle well.

**Correction cues:**
- Cue "screw the feet into the floor" or "spread the floor apart" - actively
  pushing the knees outward against a light resistance band placed just above
  the knees during warm-up sets builds the right motor pattern.
- Check stance width first - a stance that's too narrow for the lifter's hips
  makes tracking-out mechanically harder regardless of muscular effort.
- If valgus only appears on the last rep or two of a set, it is a fatigue
  pattern, not a mobility problem - the fix is reducing volume or load, not
  more mobility work.
- Slow the descent down (a 2-3 second eccentric) - valgus collapse is far
  more common on a fast, uncontrolled drop than on a controlled descent.
