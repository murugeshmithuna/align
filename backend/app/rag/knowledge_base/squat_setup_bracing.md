# Squat Setup and Bracing

**What it looks like:** Problems that trace back to before the rep even
starts - foot stance too narrow or too wide for the lifter's hip structure,
toes pointed too far forward (restricting hip rotation) or too far out
(losing knee-tracking control), or no real brace taken before descending, so
the trunk is soft and reactive rather than rigid.

**Why it happens:** Setup habits are often copied from a generic cue ("feet
shoulder width, toes straight") without adjusting for individual hip anatomy,
and bracing is easy to skip once a lifter is comfortable with a weight,
especially in fatigue.

**Why it matters:** A poor setup is frequently the root cause behind
downstream issues that look like separate problems - narrow stance
contributing to knee valgus, weak bracing contributing to back rounding,
toes-forward stance limiting achievable depth. Fixing the setup often
resolves two or three flagged issues at once instead of training each
symptom in isolation.

**Correction cues:**
- Start stance width at "outside shoulder width" and adjust from there based
  on what lets the lifter reach full depth with knees tracking cleanly over
  the toes - there's no universally correct width.
- Turn the toes out roughly 10-20 degrees as a starting point; more than
  that trades knee stability for hip range, less than that restricts depth.
- Take a full breath into the belly (not the chest) and brace the abdominal
  wall like expecting a punch, before every single rep - not just the first
  rep of a set.
- If multiple form flags (depth, knee tracking, back angle) show up together
  on the same session, check setup and bracing first before treating them as
  three separate problems.
