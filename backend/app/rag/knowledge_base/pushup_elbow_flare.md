# Push-Up Elbow Flare

**What it looks like:** The elbows point out close to 90 degrees from the
torso during the descent, forming a "T" shape with the arms, instead of
tracking back at a narrower angle (commonly cued as 30-45 degrees from the
torso).

**Why it happens:** Often just an unexamined default technique, but it can
also come from a very wide hand placement that mechanically forces the
elbows outward, or from trying to bias the chest over the triceps without
realizing the shoulder cost.

**Why it matters:** Wide elbow flare puts the shoulder into a compromised,
high-stress position at end-range, particularly under repeated load - it's
one of the more common technical contributors to shoulder impingement-type
discomfort from push-ups over time.

**Correction cues:**
- Narrow the hand placement slightly and cue the elbows to track back at a
  diagonal, brushing lightly against the sides of the torso rather than
  winging straight out.
- Imagine pushing the body away from the floor at an angle, not straight
  down - this naturally encourages the narrower elbow path.
- If flare only appears once fatigue sets in, it usually means the working
  rep count is exceeding what strict form can currently support - reducing
  volume preserves the correct movement pattern better than pushing through.
