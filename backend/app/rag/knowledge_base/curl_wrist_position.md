# Bicep Curl Wrist Position

**What it looks like:** The wrist flexes (curls forward) or extends
(cocks backward) during the lift instead of staying in a neutral, straight
line with the forearm - most visible at the top of the rep where grip
strength is most challenged.

**Why it happens:** Grip strength lagging behind bicep strength is the most
common cause - as the biceps successfully lift a weight the hands can't
fully control, the wrist breaks position to compensate. It can also come from
gripping the bar too far forward or back in the palm.

**Why it matters:** A flexed or extended wrist under load is a well-known
contributor to wrist strain and, over time, tendon irritation - the wrist
joint is not designed to be a load-bearing hinge the way the elbow is.

**Correction cues:**
- Grip the bar or dumbbell in the middle of the palm, not high in the
  fingers - a higher grip gives the wrist more mechanical leverage to stay
  neutral under load.
- Cue "wrist stays straight, like a hammer" throughout the entire rep, top
  to bottom.
- If wrist position only breaks down on the heaviest sets, treat it as a
  grip-strength limiter and consider wrist wraps or a lighter working weight
  rather than accepting the compensation.
